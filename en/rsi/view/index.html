My take · RSI from an SA's perspective

After reviewing RSI Landscape, Investment perspective, AlphaEvolve Explained and those papers, the author's own conclusions.Everything on this page is opinion, not fact; each one lists its basis and confidence level, so you can go back to the original sources if you disagree. Compiled 2026-11-18.

In short:RSI is real, but what enterprises can use today is its "small version"— Freeze the model; automatically modify the harness and algorithms. Generation is no longer scarce,What's scarce is trustworthy evaluation. The most valuable thing an SA can do is help customers build "how to score" first, and only then talk about self-improvement.
Confidence:High Multiple primary sources point to the same conclusion;Medium Evidence exists, but may change;Low Mostly speculation; worth continued watching.

① Assessment of the field

  • "AI helping build AI" is already happening, but "AI building AI on its own" is still a long way off.High
    Basis: over 75% of Google's production code is written by AI, but about 88% still requires repeated human guidance; METR estimates Anthropic's R&D speed at about 2x; OpenAI says explicitly that fully autonomous RSI should not be pursued now. The three frontier labs point in the same direction, differing only in pace.
  • What enterprises can actually deploy is L2 in Figure 5: humans set the goals and scoring, and AI chooses the changes.High
    Basis: Products from Google, Microsoft and AWS (AlphaEvolve, Foundry Agent Optimizer, AgentCore optimization) all stop at this level; L3 and above exist only in research. When customers say "self-evolving," nine times out of ten they mean this level.
  • The bottleneck is verification, not generation.High
    Basis: In this demo, 97.7% of the cost per round was evaluation; AlphaEvolve's generation is hosted, while evaluation runs on the customer's side; nearly all new papers (SoL-Pi, TRUST, HarnessDev) are tackling "how to verify."
  • Real gains are mostly in the single- to double-digit percentage range. It's not magic.High
    Basis: FM Logistic 10.4%, Coolblue 5%, JetBrains 15–20%, Google's skill optimization about 5%, Borg 0.7%. On systems that are already highly optimized, these gains are very valuable; but with customers, talk about "verifiable small wins," not "self-evolving AI."
  • Investor enthusiasm is running ahead of technical progress.Medium
    Basis: Several new labs with no revenue are valued at $4 billion to $50 billion, mostly on self-reported results; a year from now, some may pivot or be acquired. Hype itself is not evidence.

② How to pitch it to customers

  • Diagnose first: three questions High:
    1. Do you have Can be scored automatically method (tests, benchmarks, business metrics)?
    2. Do you have Held-out set(problems the optimization process has never seen)?
    3. Before changes go live Who reviews, can it be rolled back?
    None of the three: build evals first and don't talk about self-improvement. One: run a small-scope PoC. All three: you can adopt an automated optimization loop.
  • Pick problems that "can be scored" for the PoC.High
    Good fit: performance optimization (kernels, queries, video processing), scheduling and routing, demand forecasting, supply chain simulation, chip verification, and agent prompts / skills. Poor fit: problems with no benchmark, where quality depends on subjective judgment, or where feedback takes months to arrive.
  • Change the harness first, not the weights.High
    Basis: It only runs inference, is cheap and can be rolled back at any time; the demo's 0.42 → 0.94 was achieved with frozen weights. Leave weight changes (fine-tuning, RL) until the harness has no more headroom.
  • Build "anti-gaming" into the proposal.High
    Basis: in Google's video tutorial, the loop "skips rendering to get faster"; Weco measured 63% reward hacking. The solution needs: hidden problems, re-testing with rephrased prompts, capability floors, cost penalties, and human review.
  • Don't use the term "RSI" to sell solutions.Medium
    Basis: In the US it is already a sensitive term for regulators and the public (a bill to ban it has been proposed); for enterprise customers, "automated optimization + evaluation loop" is more accurate and easier to get through internal approval.

③ How to view the competition

  • Acknowledge that Google leads in "managed algorithm discovery."High
    Basis: AlphaEvolve is GA, with public case studies from BASF, Klarna, JetBrains, and others, plus Cloud Batch fan-out evals; Microsoft and AWS currently have no equivalent managed service.
  • Don't compete with AlphaEvolve on the engine; compete on the closed loop and neutrality.Medium
    Why: The evolution engine has mature open-source implementations (ShinkaEvolve, OpenEvolve, with the same structure as AlphaEvolve). What customers actually lack is evaluation, pre-launch validation (offline evaluation + A/B), a choice of models, and integration with existing systems. AWS AgentCore optimization / Evaluations fits right at this layer.
  • "Generate in the cloud, evaluate on your side" works for everyone.Medium
    Why: AlphaEvolve's evaluations already run on the customer's own compute. You can build the same structure with an open-source engine + Bedrock + AWS Batch / Spot, and evaluation is often the biggest cost.
  • Microsoft's product loop is well refined; don't underestimate it.Medium
    Basis: Foundry's "production traces → human labeling → optimization → human PR review → regression set → canary rollout" is the most complete reference design of the three.

④ Infrastructure opportunities

  • Evaluation compute is a new kind of workload.High
    Characteristics: many small, parallel, interruptible tasks, with one sandbox per candidate that is discarded after it runs. This suits Spot, Batch, and fast-booting microVMs. Google built a dedicated Agent Compute Engine, which shows the big players see it the same way.
  • RSI companies need more than GPUs.Medium
    Basis: Experiment orchestration, sandboxes, evaluation clusters, experiment log storage and fast delivery of the latest accelerators; automated kernel optimization may also make models easier to port to non-CUDA chips.
  • Follow the funding to see demand.Medium
    Basis: New labs usually sign their main compute contracts 1–3 months after raising, and a large share of the first round goes to a single cloud (about 63% for Recursive, about half for Mirendil). Funding news is a demand signal.
  • Governance shifts from a "burden" to a "requirement".Medium
    Basis: Regulatory proposals require auditing, isolation and kill switches; labs and researchers are doing this themselves too (OpenAI monitors full trajectories for new models; NVIDIA SoL-Pi never feeds held-out results back to the optimizer). Platforms that turn these into ready-made capabilities will sell better.

⑤ Assessment for the China Regions

  • Domestic RSI startups are fairly far from overseas clouds.Medium
    Basis: Mostly angel / Pre-A rounds, funded mainly by local state-owned capital and USD funds; compute partners are undisclosed, and they are likely using domestic chips.
  • The more practical opportunities are with two types of customers.Medium
    First,Chinese companies expanding overseas evaluation, inference, and sandboxes; second, "Algorithm optimization" in traditional industries — supply chain, logistics, and manufacturing scheduling. This is where AlphaEvolve has the most public case studies (BASF, FM Logistic, Kinaxis), and where local customers can most easily calculate ROI.
  • Research in China is very active and worth bringing up.High
    Basis: ByteDance, Huawei, Shanghai AI Lab, DeepSeek and others all have work on harness self-evolution and self-verification (see Resource pack · Results from China), and the focus is again on "how to verify".

⑥ What I would do next

  1. Prepare an "evaluation-first" demo: use the existing demo to explain where 0.42 → 0.94 comes from, focusing on evals, promotion gates, and cost rather than "AI getting smarter on its own".
  2. Find a customer problem that can be scored and run a small PoC: prioritize problems with clear metrics, such as supply chain, routing, and performance optimization, and spend the first two weeks building the evals and the held-out set.
  3. Make a competitor comparison card: what AlphaEvolve / Foundry Agent Optimizer / AgentCore optimization each change, how they verify, and where they run evals (material in Landscape ③).
  4. Update the watchlist monthly(see ⑦).

⑦ What to watch over the next six months

  • Whether Microsoft and AWS will launch a managed "algorithm discovery" service to rival AlphaEvolve.
  • How Anthropic, after going public, discloses AI R&D acceleration and RSI risk in its quarterly reports.
  • Whether third-party evaluations like METR's cover more labs, and whether the evidence becomes more transparent.
  • Whether US bills to ban or regulate RSI actually move forward.
  • Whether new labs can deliver Independently verifiable results, not just self-reported benchmarks.
  • Whether the gains in enterprise cases are growing, or staying in the single-digit to double-digit range.

These views will be updated as new material comes in. If you see things differently or have new evidence, please let us know.
